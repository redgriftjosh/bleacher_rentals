# Preflight — 1.8.1

PR: #369
Status: passed
Signed: 2026-09-02 against e2ba4fa

A small release: Canadian quotes get their tax filled in, and two warnings appear
in the quote builder. No migration, no schema change, no new page, and nothing
that changes who can do what.

The Canadian rate table and the email-domain check are covered by unit tests, and
a route test now proves a Canadian address never contacts QuickBooks. What is
left below is the part only a person can judge — that the numbers show up in the
right place on screen, and that the US path still works against a real QuickBooks
company.

## New in this release

- [x] **Quotes & Bookings →** new quote **→** pick a **Canadian** sales office
      and an **Ontario** event address. The Totals box shows **Tax (13.00%)** and
      the total matches 13% of the subtotal.
- [x] Change the event address to a **Quebec** one. The label reads
      **Tax (14.975%)** — all three decimals, not 14.97 or 14.98.
- [x] Change it to an **Alberta** address — **Tax (5.00%)**. Then try a province
      with no QuickBooks tax code in the file (BC, say): the rate still appears.
- [x] Save that Canadian quote, reload the edit page, and confirm the tax line
      comes back with the same rate and amount.
- [x] Send the Canadian quote to yourself and open the link. The tax percentage
      and amount on the **public quote page** and in the **PDF** match what the
      builder showed.
- [x] Pick a **US** sales office with a **Canadian** event address. A red banner
      appears above the form saying they are in different countries, the tax line
      is blank, and no tax is guessed.
- [x] Do the reverse — **Canadian** office, **US** event address. Same banner,
      with the wording the other way round. Fix the address and the banner clears
      and tax calculates.
- [x] Override the tax by hand on a Canadian quote, then press **Reset to
      calculated tax**. It goes back to the province rate, not to blank.
- [x] **Quotes & Bookings →** new quote **→ Account Manager.** Pick someone whose
      email is not `@bleacherrentals.com`. Red text appears under the dropdown
      naming the address. Switch to a company-email AM and it disappears.

## Could have broken

- [x] **A US quote still gets its tax from QuickBooks.** Pick a US office and a
      US event address with a real tax rate — the correct percentage appears.
      This is the path most likely to have been disturbed, since the Canadian
      branch sits in front of it.
- [x] **The currency label on the sales office dropdown.** Each office still
      shows `(CAD)` or `(USD)` correctly, and picking one still sets the quote's
      currency. The province list behind this was moved to a shared file.
- [x] **The existing QuickBooks error banner.** Pick a sales office with no
      QuickBooks connection — the old "Failed to load tax data" banner still
      appears, and does not collide with the new cross-border one.

## Not testing

- **The province rates themselves** — all 13 provinces and territories, the
  Quebec GST+QST combination, and name-vs-code matching ("Ontario" vs "ON") are
  covered by `canadianTaxRates.test.ts`. Checking each by hand would be 13 items
  for no added confidence.
- **That a Canadian address never calls QuickBooks** — asserted directly in
  `route.test.ts`, including that no access token is even fetched. The manual
  items above check the number is right, not how it was obtained.
- **The email domain rule** — `companyEmail.test.ts` covers case, whitespace, a
  missing address, and lookalike domains like
  `josh@bleacherrentals.com.attacker.net`.
- **Permissions** — no role's capabilities changed. The tax endpoint sits behind
  the same `requireAdminOrAccountManager` gate it had before, and this PR adds no
  new database write, so there is no RLS policy to re-check. `permissionPageData.ts`
  needs no edit.
- **The `supabase/config.toml` change** — the local email test server was renamed
  from `[inbucket]` to `[local_smtp]`. Local development only; invisible in
  production.
- **Whether QuickBooks agrees with our Canadian rate** — when a Canadian quote is
  eventually pushed into QuickBooks as an invoice, QBO applies its own tax code,
  which may not match the rate we displayed. Nothing in this release pushes
  invoices, so it cannot be tested here, but it is worth knowing before that work
  starts.
